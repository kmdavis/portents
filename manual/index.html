Portent — manual check

The things a test suite cannot judge. Automated tests already prove the SVG and the ASCII describe the same grid, cell for cell. What they cannot tell you is whether the picture looks like what the text says — whether a door reads as a door, or whether a lit room looks like something a person could stand in.

If a picture and its text disagree anywhere below, that is a bug worth reporting, because the test suite believes they match.

1. A generated dungeon

Connectivity is carved first as a spanning tree, then a tile is chosen to match each cell's required shape. So the dungeon is connected by construction, not by luck. Every room should be walkable from the entrance, and no door should open onto solid rock. The check below re-derives both projections and compares them.

2. What the party can see

Click a cell in the picture to move the party there. Unknown cells are not drawn at all; explored-but-unseen cells are dimmed; only cells in sight right now show tokens. Worth poking at: stand beside a pillar and check you can see past it on both sides; stand in a doorway and check you cannot see along the wall you are standing in; walk into a room and back out, and check the terrain is remembered but the goblin is not.

3. PNG

The same view, rasterised in the browser with no dependencies: SVG string to blob, blob to canvas, canvas to PNG. This is the path a web front end would use to show a map as an image. The PNG should be indistinguishable from the SVG above it apart from being crisper or softer depending on the scale.

4. A range of tiles

Cropping to a rectangle of tiles, for handing a player one room at a time rather than the whole map. Coordinates stay in map space, so a click on a crop still maps back to the right cell. Opens on the entrance tile; move the crop somewhere the party has not been and it correctly goes blank.

5. Every cell kind

One cell of each kind, drawn and written. Check the glyph is a sensible mnemonic, the shape is recognisable at small sizes, and the movement and sight columns match intuition — a chasm should block movement but not sight, a closed door the reverse.

6. Every tile in the pack

Picture and text side by side. All 7×7, doors dead centre on each wall. The shape label is the tile's connection signature, which is what the generator matches on.

7. Dice

Formatting check: dropped dice struck through, labels carried, totals bold.
