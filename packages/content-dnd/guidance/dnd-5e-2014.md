# D&D 5E — 2014 printing

This is the printing in play. State it in one clause when you confirm the setup, and do not drift to the other one.

## What is specific to this printing

- The original 2014 Player's Handbook. Race grants ability modifiers, backgrounds do
  not. Surprise costs the whole first turn. No weapon mastery.
- If the player says a bare "5e" they most likely mean the 2024 rules; this printing was
  chosen deliberately, so do not drift back toward it.

## In combat

- Action, bonus action, movement, one free object interaction, reaction.
- Advantage/disadvantage: `2d20kh1` and `2d20kl1`. Use these liberally as your main
  dial instead of inventing situational modifiers.
- Death saves: three failures dies, three successes stabilises, a natural 20 is back up
  at 1 HP. These are the player's own rolls, never the GM's. Record the running count
  in the sheet's `Death Saves` status key after each one.
- Concentration: when the player takes damage while concentrating, ask for a CON save
  at DC 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher. Track what they are concentrating on
  in the sheet's Status block so you remember to ask.
- Crits: double the dice, not the modifier. Optionally draw `crit-hits` instead.

## Sidekicks

The published sidekick rules live in supplements rather than the free reference
document, so they are not reproduced here. If the player owns them, use them and say so.
Otherwise build one in original terms, which is quick:

- Take a monster stat block of roughly a quarter the party's level, or a commoner for a
  non-combatant, and give it a class-flavoured trick: a second attack, a healing touch a
  few times a day, or one cantrip and one first-level spell.
- Give it the proficiency bonus of the main character's level, and hit points around
  half theirs. Level it when they level.
- One action and one bonus action, like anyone else. No Divine Smite, no
  action-surge-scale features; a sidekick should never out-damage the character.
- Advantage and disadvantage do the work of any fiddly modifier you are tempted to
  invent for it.

Two things to hold to. A sidekick makes death saves like anyone else, and dropping one
should frighten the player. And a sidekick with a spell list still gets asked, not
assumed -- if the player wants to decide whether it burns its last slot, let them.
