# Pathfinder 2E — legacy

This is the printing in play. State it in one clause when you confirm the setup, and do not drift to the other one.

## What is specific to this printing

- The 2019 Core Rulebook. Alignment is in play, schools of magic exist, damage is
  negative/positive, and spells have their original names.
- Chosen deliberately over the remaster, so do not quietly modernise a rule.

## In combat

- Three actions per turn, plus a reaction. Most things cost one action; casting usually
  costs two.
- **Multiple attack penalty:** second attack in a turn is at -5, third at -10 (-4/-8 with
  agile). This is the main reason PF2E turns are interesting; enforce it.
- **Four degrees of success:** beat the DC by 10 for a critical success, fail by 10 for a
  critical failure. A natural 20 improves the degree by one step, a natural 1 worsens it.
  The dice tool reports natural 20s and 1s, so you can apply this without guessing.
- Dying/Wounded: dying increases with each hit while down; wounded makes the next one
  worse. Track both in the Status block.
- Hero points: one at the start, more for good play. Spending one rerolls a check;
  spending all of them recovers from dying. Remind the player they have them.

## Companions and sidekicks

The action economy is the reason to have one. Three actions each means a lone character
facing three enemies is giving up nine actions a round to its three, and the multiple
attack penalty means it cannot simply swing more.

Both printings already ship several ways to add a body without a second full sheet:
an animal companion, a familiar with the right abilities, or an eidolon for a player who
wants a genuine second combatant. Prefer those to a bespoke sidekick, because they are
balanced against the encounter maths already.

Where a full ally is wanted, use a creature two or three levels below the party and
give it a single signature action. Keep its turn to one or two actions in practice --
not because the rules say so, but because a companion taking a full three-action turn
every round doubles the length of every fight.

Degrees of success apply to companions exactly as to anyone else, and a critical
failure on a companion's strike is a good moment to let the fiction bite.
